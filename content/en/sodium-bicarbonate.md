## Key points

- **We recommend to contact your poison centre with the use of this antidote.**
- In the event of salicylate intoxication, it will not be possible to alkalinize the urine if serum potassium is below 3.5 - 4 mmol/L.
- Blood pH must be monitored; it should not exceed 7.55.
- In case of shortage, consider using sodium acetate or sodium chloride as described in their respective monographs.

## Synonyms and other terms

- NaHCO3
- Sodium hydrogen carbonate

## Indications

**Cardiotoxicity:**

- **​**The following signs and symptoms caused by sodium channel blockers, such as tricyclic antidepressants, antiarrhythmic drugs (class "Ia" or "Ic") etc.&nbsp;
  - QRS widening with right ventricular delay (shown on the ECG by a prominent R wave in aVR lead and a deep S wave in lead 1; [see ECG examples](https://www.ciusss-capitalenationale.gouv.qc.ca/sites/d8/files/docs/ProfSante/Antidotes/right_ventricular_delay_ecg_morphology_examples.pdf)), **and/or**
  - Hypotension, **and/or**
  - Ventricular arrhythmia&nbsp;

**Blood alkalinization** to reduce the distribution of salicylates in the CNS.

**Urinary alkalization** due to salicylate intoxication to increase elimination.

**Correction of metabolic acidosis** caused by methanol or ethylene glycol intoxication or secondary to toxin-associated lactic acidosis.

## Dosage

### Pediatric Dose

- **Newborns, infants and children under 2 years of age:**
  - **Use a 4.2% solution (0.5 mmol/ml).**
  - **Do not administer by direct IV to newborns or infants.**
  - **Maximum concentration: 0.5 mmol/ml. Maximum of 8 mmol/kg/day.**
- **Child 2 years of age and over:**
  - **Use a 7.5% solution (0.89 mmol/ml) or a 8.4% solution (1 mmol/ml).**
- **Cardiotoxicity** :
  - 1 - 2 mmol/kg by direct IV or slow IV infusion (if newborn or infant). Repeat as needed until a blood pH of 7.45 - 7.55.
- **Blood alkalinization** :
  - 1 - 2 mmol/kg by direct IV or slow IV infusion (if newborn or infant). Follow this with urinary alkalinization dosing to maintain blood pH at approximately 7.50.
- **Urinary alkalinization**  :
  - 150 mmol in 1000 ml of D5W (or other compatible low-sodium solution) by continuous IV infusion.
  - Start infusion at 2 - 3 ml/kg/h (max: 150 - 200 ml/h) and titrate according to urinary pH.
  - Aim for urinary pH greater than 7.5
  - Blood pH and serum potassium to be closely monitored and considered when titrating the infusion.
  - The addition of KCL to an IV solution administered via a second line may be necessary to maintain serum potassium at approximately 4 mmol/L.
- **Correction of metabolic acidosis :**
  - 1 mmol/kg by slow IV infusion. Repeat as needed until blood pH is greater or equal to 7.2.

### Adult Dose

- **Use a 7.5% (0.89 mmol/ml) or a 8.4% (1 mmol/ml) solution.**
- **Cardiotoxicity :**
  - 1 - 2 mmol/kg by direct IV. Repeat as needed until a blood pH of 7.45 - 7.55.
- **Blood alkalinization :**
  - 1 - 2 mmol/kg by direct IV. Follow this with urinary alkalinization dosing to maintain blood pH at approximately 7.50.
- **Urinary alkalinization :**
  - 150 mmol in 1000 ml of D5W (or other compatible low-sodium solution) by continuous IV infusion.
  - Start infusion at 2 - 3 ml/kg/h (max: 150 - 200 ml/h) and titrate according to urinary pH.
  - Aim for urinary pH greater than 7.5
  - Blood pH and serum potassium to be closely monitored and considered when titrating the infusion.&nbsp;
  - The addition of KCL to an IV solution administered via a second line may be necessary to maintain serum potassium at approximately 4 mmol/L.
- **Correction of metabolic acidosis :**
  - 1 mmol/kg by direct IV. Repeat as needed until blood pH is greater or equal to 7.2.

### Renal Impairment

- Watch out for fluid overload (high sodium content).
- No data suggests that the dose should be modified in the setting of renal impairment.

### Hepatic Impairment

No data suggests that the dose should be modified for short-term use.

### Hemodialysis Patient

No data suggests that the dose should be modified for short-term use.

### Pregnancy

- The safety of large doses of sodium bicarbonate has not been demonstrated.
- Do not hesitate to use during pregnancy if the anticipated toxic effects pose a significant risk of morbidity or mortality.
- No data suggests that the dose should be modified for short-term use.

### Obese or Overweight Patient

No data suggests that the dose should be modified for short-term use.

## Adverse effects

- Hypernatremia (50 ml NaHCO3 at 8.4% contain 1.15 g of Na), hypokalemia, fluid overload, excessive alkalinization.
- Extravasation: local ischemia, tissue necrosis, ulceration.

## Monitoring

- Vital signs
- Serum electrolytes
- Serum calcium
- Blood gases
- Urinary pH

## End of treatment

- **Cardiotoxicity :** When QRS is less than 100 milliseconds or when blood pressure is stabilized and wide-complex dysrhythmias are corrected.
- **Blood alkalinization :** When blood pH is 7.45 - 7.55.
- **Urinary alkalinization :**
  - When major clinical symptoms have resolved (except tinnitus), and;
  - the salicylates levels have decreased for two consecutive readings, and;
  - is less than 2.5 mmol/L (345.25 mg/L)
- **Correction of metabolic acidosis :** When blood pH is greater than 7.20.

## Special Notes on Administration

**Intravenous Route (IV)**

- **WARNING** : **Rinse tubing before and after administering sodium bicarbonate to prevent incompatibilities and the inactivation of other drugs.**
- **Direct IV** :
  - **Newborns or Infants:** Not the recommended administration route.
  - **Children 2 and under:** Only in situations of resuscitation. Use a solution of 4.2% (0.5 mmol/ml) or dilute a solution of 8.4% (1 mmol/ml) in SWFI at a ratio of 1:1 in order to obtain a final concentration of 0.5 mmol/ml. Administer by direct IV at a maximum rate of 1 mmol/kg/min. without exceeding 10 mmol/min.
  - **Children 2 and over:** Only in situations of resuscitation Use a solution of 7.5% (0.89 mmol/ml) or 8.4% (1 mmol/ml). Administer by direct IV at a maximum rate of 1 mmol/kg/min. without exceeding 10 mmol/min.&nbsp;
  - **Adults:** Use a solution of 7.5% (0.89 mmol/ml) or 8.4% (1 mmol/ml). Administer by direct IV over 1 - 2 min. at a maximum rate of 1 mmol/kg/min.
- **Slow IV Infusion :**
  - Administer using a volumetric pump or syringe pump.
  - **Pediatric Dose:** Dilute dose in a compatible solution in order to obtain a final concentration inferior or equal to 0.5 mmol/ml. Administer at a maximum rate of 1 mmol/kg/h without exceeding 50 mmol/h.
  - **Adult Dose:** Dilute dose in a compatible solution in order to obtain a final concentration inferior or equal to 0.5 mmol/ml. Administer over 4 - 8 hours or at a maximum rate of 50 mmol/h.
- **Continuous IV Infusion:**&nbsp;
  - Recommended only for urinary alkalinization.
  - Administer using a volumetric pump.
  - <strong><u>Formula using a solution with a concentration of 7.5% (0.89 mmol/ml):</u></strong>
    - 134 mmol (150 ml) in 1000 ml of a compatible solution.
    - Final volume: 1150 ml.
    - Final concentration: 0.12 mmol/ml.
  - <u><strong>Formula using a solution with a concentration of 8.4% (1 mmol/ml):</strong></u>
    - 150 mmol (150 ml) in 1000 ml of a compatible solution.
    - Final volume: 1150 ml.
    - Final concentration: 0.13 mmol/ml.

**Subcutaneous Route (SC)**

- Do not administer by this route.

**Intramuscular Route (IM)**

- Do not administer by this route.

**Intraosseous Route (IO)**

- Possible alternative to IV route.

- **Incompatible with acids, acidic salts and lactate solutions.**

**Stability**

- Store undiluted products at room temperature (20°C - 25°C).
- Diluted with compatible solutions in PVC bag: no data. Consider maximum stability to be 24 h.
- Diluted in D5W polyolefin bag: stable for 30 h at room temperature.
